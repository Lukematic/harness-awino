PROCEDURE osmani-idea-refine (sharpen a vague idea into a spec-ready concept; DEFINE phase):
POSITIONING — THIS SKILL RUNS AFTER THE DISCOVERY INTERVIEW AND BEFORE MISSION-DEFINITION. It does not duplicate the interview: it TAKES the interview output as input. The discovery grill (discovery) asks what the user wants; idea-refine takes that raw material and stress-tests it into a sharp, actionable concept the spec can be written from. Entry condition: an interview transcript or equivalent raw idea. Exit condition: a one-pager the user confirmed, ready to become a mission contract.

1. DIVERGENT — UNDERSTAND AND EXPAND: restate the idea as a crisp "How Might We" problem statement (forces clarity on what is actually being solved). Ask 3-5 sharpening questions ONLY on what the interview left unresolved — no more, and never re-ask what the interview already answered: who is this for, specifically; what does success look like; what are the real constraints; what has been tried before; why now. Then generate 5-8 idea variations through explicit lenses: inversion (what if we did the opposite); constraint removal; audience shift; combination with an adjacent idea; the 10x-simpler version; the 10x-scale version; the domain-expert lens (what would experts find obvious). Inside a codebase: ground variations in the existing architecture (scan it first) — the architecture is both constraint and opportunity.
2. CONVERGENT — EVALUATE AND STRESS-TEST: after the user reacts, cluster what resonated into 2-3 MEANINGFULLY DIFFERENT directions (not variations on a theme). Stress-test each against: user value (painkiller or vitamin — who benefits, how much); feasibility (technical and resource cost — what is the hardest part); differentiation (what is genuinely different — would someone switch). Surface hidden assumptions for each direction: what you are betting is true but have not validated; what could kill this idea; what you are choosing to ignore and why that is acceptable for now. Untested assumptions are the #1 killer of good ideas — this step is not skippable. BE HONEST, NOT SUPPORTIVE: a weak idea gets pushback with specificity and kindness. (This is the devil's-advocate posture applied to ideation; see osmani-doubt for the formal per-decision procedure.)
3. SHARPEN — PRODUCE THE ONE-PAGER: a concrete markdown artifact (the thing that feeds mission-definition):
   # [Idea Name] / Problem Statement (one-sentence How Might We) / Recommended Direction (why, 2-3 paragraphs max) / Key Assumptions to Validate (each with how to test it) / MVP Scope (minimum version that tests the core assumption — in AND out) / Not Doing list (each with WHY — focus is saying no to good ideas; this list is arguably the most valuable part) / Open Questions (what needs answering before building).
   The user confirms the direction before anything is written into a contract. Save only on confirmation.
