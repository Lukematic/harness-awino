PROCEDURE osmani-doubt (subject every non-trivial decision to fresh-context adversarial review; cross-phase guardrail, declared at PLAN):
A confident answer is not a correct one. Long sessions quietly turn assumptions into "facts" — this skill materializes a reviewer biased to DISPROVE, not approve, before any non-trivial output stands. Doubt is in-flight: cross-examination happens while course-correction is still cheap, not at the end.

STANCE VS SKILL — THE DISTINCTION THAT MAKES THIS WORK. Devil's-advocate is a turn-level STANCE: a posture held during one turn (the VERIFY turn attacks claims), with no steps and no exit criteria of its own. Doubt-driven development is a WORKFLOW SKILL: a five-step procedure (below) with explicit exit criteria, executable in any phase wherever the stakes justify it. They COMPOSE: the stance supplies the adversarial attitude, the skill supplies the procedure. A VERIFY turn can hold devil's-advocate posture AND run a doubt cycle inside it. Posture without procedure is unfocused; procedure without posture is checkbox theater. Neither replaces the other.

1. CLAIM: name the decision in 2-3 lines + why it matters. "The new caching layer is thread-safe under the read-heavy workload in the spec. WHY: a race here corrupts user data and is hard to detect in QA." If you cannot write it that compactly, you have a vibe, not a decision — surface it before scrutinizing it. A decision is NON-TRIVIAL when it introduces/modifies branching logic, crosses a module boundary, asserts something types cannot verify (thread safety, idempotence, ordering), depends on context the future reader cannot see, or has an irreversible blast radius. NOT for: mechanical renames, unambiguous user instructions, one-line obvious changes, pure tooling.
2. EXTRACT: the smallest reviewable unit — artifact + contract, NOT the journey. Code: the diff or function, not the whole file. Decision: the proposal in 3-5 sentences plus the constraints it must satisfy. Assertion: the claim plus the evidence — kept distinct from the Step 1 CLAIM (the hypothesis under scrutiny). Strip your reasoning: hand over conclusions and you get validation of your conclusions back.
3. DOUBT: invoke the fresh-context reviewer with an adversarial prompt — "Find what is wrong with this artifact. Assume the author is overconfident. Look for: unstated assumptions; unhandled edge cases; hidden coupling or shared state; ways the contract could be violated; conventions it might break; failure modes under unexpected input. Do NOT validate. Do NOT summarize." Pass ARTIFACT + CONTRACT ONLY — never the CLAIM (biases toward agreement), never your reasoning. Awino mechanics: spawn a subagent with exactly this prompt and those two inputs; a fresh context is the load-bearing property. A failing test produced by TDD's RED step IS the doubt step for behavioral claims (the disproof is executable). Degraded fallback when no fresh context is possible: rewrite artifact + contract as a fresh self-prompt with a hard mental separator from prior reasoning, flag the result as DEGRADED — you carry your own context with you. Cross-model: in interactive sessions, offering a second-opinion review (different model/tooling) is MANDATORY after every single-model review — the user decides whether the artifact warrants the cost. Non-interactive: skip, and ANNOUNCE the skip. Never invoke external tooling without explicit user authorization.
4. RECONCILE: the reviewer's output is data, not verdict — you remain the orchestrator. Re-read the artifact text against each finding (rubber-stamping the reviewer is the same failure as ignoring it) and classify, first match wins: (a) CONTRACT MISREAD — the finding came from an unclear/incomplete contract: fix the contract first; (b) VALID + ACTIONABLE — real issue: change the artifact, re-loop; (c) VALID TRADE-OFF — real but costs more to fix than accept: document it explicitly so the user sees it (see osmani-failure-modes: unexamined trade-offs are a failure mode); (d) NOISE — correct under context the reviewer lacked: note it, and ask whether the contract should have carried that context. Doubt theater (checkable signal): two or more cycles with substantive findings and ZERO classified actionable — you are validating, not doubting. Stop and escalate.
5. STOP: bounded loop, not recursion. Stop when the next iteration returns only trivial/already-considered findings, OR 3 cycles are complete (escalate to the user — a fourth cycle alone is grinding; three unresolved cycles is information about the artifact), OR the user explicitly says ship it. Artifact too big for 3 cycles means the artifact is too big — decompose in Step 2 and re-run, do not lift the bound. Never doubt an unchanged artifact twice (you will get the same findings; you are stalling).
